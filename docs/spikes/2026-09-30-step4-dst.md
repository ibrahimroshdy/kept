# Spike V21: Egypt's DST in 2026 (step 4, T0)

Date: 2026-09-30. Step-4 plan, Task 0 (V21). Result: **Node and Postgres agree.** `Africa/Cairo`
springs forward at local midnight on Friday 24 April 2026 and falls back at local midnight going
into Friday 30 October 2026. T14's DST tests use these computed instants, and a test recomputes
them rather than hard-coding them.

Code: `docs/spikes/code/step4/dst/transitions.mjs` (`node transitions.mjs [zone] [year]`) and
`transitions.sql` (`psql -At -F ' | ' -f transitions.sql`, against any Postgres; the spike used the
dev database).

Both are computed from the tz data, never typed in:

- **Node:** scan the year's `Intl.DateTimeFormat` offsets every 15 minutes, then binary-search each
  change to the second;
- **Postgres:** scan every minute with `AT TIME ZONE`.

## Versions

- **Node:** v24.21.0, ICU 78.3, tz **2026a** (`process.versions`).
- **Postgres:** 18.6, on the dev container `kept-dev-db-1` (Debian, aarch64). Its tz data is the
  image's; the result agrees with Node's 2026a.

## The transitions

| Instant (UTC) | Offset | Local wall clock |
|---|---|---|
| **2026-04-23 22:00:00Z** | +02:00 → +03:00 (EET → EEST) | 23:59:59 on Thu 23 Apr is followed by **01:00:00 on Fri 24 Apr**: 00:00–00:59 on 24 April doesn't exist, and 24 April has 23 hours |
| **2026-10-29 21:00:00Z** | +03:00 → +02:00 (EEST → EET) | 23:59:59 EEST on Thu 29 Oct is followed by **23:00:00 EET on Thu 29 Oct**: 23:00–23:59 on 29 October happens twice, and 29 October has 25 hours |

Node printed exactly these, as `2026-04-23T22:00:00.000Z GMT+02:00 -> GMT+03:00` and
`2026-10-29T21:00:00.000Z GMT+03:00 -> GMT+02:00`. Postgres returned the same two rows from
`lag()` over `(t AT TIME ZONE 'Africa/Cairo') - (t AT TIME ZONE 'UTC')`.

## How Postgres reads the awkward wall times (for T14)

| Query | Result | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `'2026-04-24 00:30'::timestamp AT TIME ZONE 'Africa/Cairo'` (doesn't exist) | 2026-04-23 22:30Z | read as 01:30 EEST: **pushed forward** by the gap |
| `'2026-10-29 23:30'::timestamp AT TIME ZONE 'Africa/Cairo'` (happens twice) | 2026-10-29 21:30Z | read as 23:30 **EET**: the **second, later** occurrence |

The location's "today", `(now() AT TIME ZONE 'Africa/Cairo')::date`:

| now() | today |
|---|---|
| 2026-04-23 21:59:59Z | 2026-04-23 |
| 2026-04-23 22:00:00Z | **2026-04-24** (midnight never shows; the day starts at 01:00) |
| 2026-10-29 20:59:59Z | 2026-10-29 |
| 2026-10-29 21:00:00Z | 2026-10-29 (the repeated hour begins) |
| 2026-10-29 21:59:59Z | 2026-10-29 |
| 2026-10-29 22:00:00Z | **2026-10-30** |

## What T14 takes from this

- **Compute the date in SQL.** "Today" and "overdue" use the location's date, which the agenda
  view already computes in SQL. The two transition days just have 23 and 25 hours, and nothing
  keyed on a date is ever skipped or doubled.
- **Digest times and quiet hours are wall-clock times in the person's zone.** A digest at
  `00:30` on 24 April doesn't exist.
  - Postgres would send it at 01:30 EEST. T14 should instead fire it the first time the local
    clock is at or past the digest time on that local date: 01:00. The rule keys on the local
    date, "one digest per user, local day and channel" (T7's `notification_digests`), so it is
    never missed.
  - A `23:30` digest on 29 October happens twice; the same key sends it once.
  - The tests are: a digest time inside the spring gap is sent once that day; a digest time
    inside the autumn overlap is sent once; a quiet-hours window across either transition ends
    at the right instant.
- **Pick one reading of ambiguous wall times.** If JavaScript ever turns a wall time into an
  instant, it must match Postgres: a nonexistent time is pushed forward, and an ambiguous one
  takes the later instant. Better, leave that conversion to SQL.
- **Month arithmetic agrees too** (Q27): `date + make_interval(months => n)` clamps to the month's
  end, as `addMonthsClamped()` does. `2026-01-31 + 1` gives 2026-02-28, `2028-01-31 + 1` gives
  2028-02-29, and `2028-02-29 + 12` gives 2029-02-28. A 24-month term from 2026-10-01 ends
  2028-09-30, as `warrantyEnds()` says.
