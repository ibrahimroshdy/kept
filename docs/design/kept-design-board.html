D135 · final · mark B, 5d41691

Logo: B · Label tape Final

A square of label-maker tape, the same object people stick on boxes, with a heavy Plex Mono K and the tape's punched hole at the top start. That pierced mark is the logo everywhere, at every size: favicon, app icons, the rail, the docs site and the README. The lockup is the mark with KEPT beside it in the same Plex Mono SemiBold, in the text colour. The tape is also the short-ID chip on every thing and on every printed label, so the brand and the label are one object.

The mark and the lockup are drawn from the font's own outlines (components/brand.tsx, scripts/render-icons.mjs), so they need no font at any size. The hole is cut out of the tape, so it shows whatever the mark sits on; amber and its ink keep their colours in both themes, and the wordmark takes the text colour.

Considered: A · Box K and C · Tag

A · Box K. A K from one continuous stroke in an amber box, like a lid opening. Recommended for small-size legibility, and not chosen.

C · Tag. A luggage tag with a punched hole: the strongest story, but it lost its hole below 32 px and read as a generic arrow at favicon size.

D133 · final

Charts

Charts are drawn with visx. Kept writes the SVG itself with visx's scales and shapes, so colours come straight from the theme tokens, every mark carries an ARIA label, and right-to-left is a mirrored scale.

Why visx

MIT, v4.0.0 (June 2026). Owning the markup makes the three D80 criteria true by construction: CSS-variable theming, accessibility, RTL. Recharts is more popular but passes colours as SVG attributes, where CSS variables don't reliably resolve.

Three series, then "Other"

On Kept's warm dark surface, only three-colour palettes pass every colourblind and contrast check in both themes. Charts show at most three coloured series; the rest fold into grey "Other" or split into small multiples.

Colours with jobs

Blue, orange, aqua for series. Amber stays brand, violet stays AI, status colours stay status, so a series is never mistaken for a warning.

Decisions that shape the UI

What every screen follows. The frames, flows and kit that apply them are on the screens board.

  1. D131
    Colour tokens

    Both themes designed and measured: warm stone neutrals, amber as a fill with dark ink (8.2:1), violet only for AI-suggested values, status colours that always travel with an icon and a word. Every text token is at least 4.5:1.

  2. D132
    Type scale

    IBM Plex in three voices: display 28/34, title 20/26, body 15/23, small 13/18, label 11.5 caps. Plex Sans Arabic at the same scale, taller lines; Plex Mono for anything checked against a label.

  3. D133
    Charts

    visx, drawn by Kept. Three series colours, then grey "Other"; a legend from two series, direct labels on notable marks, a tooltip and a table view.

  4. D134
    Component motifs

    The amber-tape short-ID chip is the one motif. Pills pair colour with icon and word; the capture mode strip is on dark chrome; the assistant is a speech bubble with a question mark.

  5. D135
    Logo

    B · Label tape: the mark is a square of tape with its punched hole and a mono K, at every size; the lockup is that mark with KEPT beside it. Chosen over A (Box K) and C (Tag).

  6. D174
    Screens specified, navigation global

    Every screen has a spec; views span all your locations with a location filter; routes use IDs. A control you can't use is hidden for your role, "Off in this location" for a module, or disabled with the reason when offline or out of budget.

  7. D175
    Interaction rules

    The camera stays open with a counter; the Inbox is Mine by default and grouped by capture batch; sections on phones, tabs on desktops; a secret shows for 30 s; assistant cards are drawn from the arguments; conflicts merge field by field; the carrying tray, box check and a label start cell.

  8. D198
    The icon rail

    From 768 px the sidebar folds to a 64 px rail with tooltips; ⌘\ toggles it, the choice is remembered per device, and 768–1023 px starts on the rail.

  9. D203
    Content width

    Centered or Full width, per device, applied before first paint. Lists and tables use the room; running text and forms keep their measure. Phones are unaffected.

  10. D204
    Languages, theme, digits

    Five languages (Arabic is right to left), a custom language menu with flags, theme System, Light or Dark, and Eastern Arabic digits offered only in Arabic.

  11. D205
    The filter strip

    One filtering component on every list: a search box, a dark chip per filter in use, + Filter (Filters (n) on phones), saved views as tabs, all in the URL.

  12. D208
    Short-ID addresses and your own codes

    A thing lives at /t/2HX9RB. Your own codes (HOME-0042) sit beside the short ID, never replacing it; the 6-character code itself is fixed because it is printed.

  13. D211
    The Display button

    Sort, direction, grouping and layout sit behind one button at the end of the strip's row; no second toolbar, nothing wraps.

  14. D212
    Pull to refresh

    Phones only. It arms after 16 px of pull at the top of the page, so it never costs a tap; off on desktops and under sheets.

  15. D213
    Speak a list, add it all at once

    The assistant turns "a drill, a ladder and two paint cans" into one confirmation card; one Confirm creates them all, one Undo takes them back.

  16. iOS
    The status strip

    In the installed iPhone app an opaque strip the height of the safe area sits at the top (paper) and, with the tab bar, at the bottom (surface), so iOS 26 tints the edges instead of blurring them (styles/index.css; no D number).
