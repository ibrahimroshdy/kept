# Step 5 performance: the agenda with vehicles, the Vehicles list, costs, fuel, the thing page and the report

This covers the "Perf" item of step 5, task 25. The check is
`apps/server/test/perf/vehicles.perf.test.ts` (full mode: `scripts/ci-local.sh perf`, or
`pnpm --filter @kept/server exec vitest run --config vitest.perf.config.ts`). It was run twice on
2026-10-06, alone (the only perf run at the time), but not on a quiet machine: other agents' test
suites ran against the same Postgres throughout. Both runs are reported. No limit was loosened.

**Verdict.**
- **The agenda with step 5's branches misses its limit, by much more than load explains.** Home's
  p95 went from 392 ms before the vehicles were added to 849 ms after (2.2×; p50 3×) in the same
  run, on the same rows. The limit is step 4's recorded p95 plus 20% (110.4 ms). The cause, inferred
  from the view and not yet measured query by query, is below.
- **The Vehicles list misses badly:** p95 1,026 ms against 200 ms (p50 330 ms) for 50 vehicles.
- **The running costs over five years, the fuel summary over 600 fills, and a thing page with 3
  meters pass** on the second run (179, 95 and 88 ms p95 against 200, 100 and 200). The first run,
  at load 20–26, failed the costs (560 ms) and the fuel summary (193 ms).
- **The five-year history report renders under the renderer's 512 MB limit:** 2.0–3.8 s, a 476 KB
  PDF (`render/render.ts` kills the child past the limit, so a render that finishes is under it).

## The fixture

Step 4's household (`test/perf/household.ts`, shared with `step4.perf.test.ts`): 10,000 things,
2,000 warranties, 500 schedules, 300 loans and 200 documents in Home. Then:

- 49 cars in Home, made through the API, each with its four starter schedules (three every N km or
  M months, brake fluid by months only) and 12 accepted odometer readings over 110 days: 150 unit
  schedules in all, each one's due date estimated through `kept.meter_estimate`/`kept.meter_eta`.
- The Corolla in the Garage (Complete, Fuel on), the 50th vehicle: 600 full fills over five years,
  each with its own `fuel` reading and a cost; 60 confirmed services with their own readings and
  two lines; 5 insurance renewals with a cost.
- A compressor in Home (no type) with 3 meters (hours, distance, a custom counter), 12 readings
  each.

Seeding the vehicles took 282–509 s (the 49 cars and their starter schedules go through the API).

## Why the agenda and the list are slow (inferred, not yet measured per query)

- `public.agenda_items`' schedule branch calls `kept.schedule_due()` for every schedule (0089).
  For a unit schedule that calls `kept.meter_latest()` and `kept.meter_eta()`, which calls
  `kept.meter_estimate()`. All are SQL functions with `SET search_path`, which Postgres never
  inlines (step 4 found the same with `kept.module_on`, fixed in 0071), so each runs as its own
  plan per row: 150 estimates per read of the view here.
- `kept.meter_estimate()` reads every accepted reading of the meter up to now (the CTE `r`) with a
  correlated `meter_events` lookup per reading, before narrowing to the 90-day window. On the
  Corolla that is 660 readings for each of its unit schedules.
- The `reading_stale` branch adds a lateral latest-reading lookup per meter.
- The Vehicles list reads `agenda_items` for each vehicle's next due point and the estimate per
  vehicle (`vehicles/list.ts`), so it pays the same costs per row.

The fix is a migration that replaces 0089's view and 0066's functions: bound `meter_estimate`'s
readings to the 90-day window by the index (`meter_readings_meter_taken_idx`), take the latest and
the earliest by index, and compute each meter's estimate once per read (a lateral join on the
distinct meters, or an inlinable function without `SET search_path`), not once per schedule. It is
carried over ([step-5-carryover.md](../plans/step-5-carryover.md)); `vehicles.perf.test.ts` keeps
its limits, so `ci-local.sh perf` fails until it lands.

## After the fix (2026-10-07: 0100, JIT off, Home's facts, one agenda read)

Measured query by query on a restore of the perf fixture, then with the perf files run alone
(`vitest.perf.config.ts`, load average 8–10, other agents' suites running). Every limit passes.

What it was, measured (no longer inferred):

- **JIT.** Every set-returning SQL function is costed at 1,000 rows a call, so the plans of the
  agenda's readers, and Home's facts query over 10,000 things (the per-thing meter lookup that
  step 5 added), passed `jit_above_cost`. Postgres JIT-compiled them on every request: 0.3–3 s,
  more than the queries themselves. Kept's connections now run with `jit=off`
  (`src/db/pools.ts`, and the test pools in `test/db.ts`), and the agenda's functions are costed
  at one row.
- **The schedule branch.** About 160 of a view read's 170 ms: `kept.schedule_due()` per schedule,
  with `kept.meter_latest()` called for every schedule and `kept.meter_eta()` →
  `kept.meter_estimate()` for each unit one, none of them inlinable (`SET search_path`). 0100 makes
  the invoker functions inlinable, reads the latest reading only for a schedule on a meter, joins
  the ETA (`kept.meter_eta_at()`) once per unit schedule, and bounds the estimate's reads by the
  index. A view read is now about 35 ms; every row is the same (compared row for row on the
  fixture: the agenda, every meter's estimate and ETA, every schedule's next point).
- **Home's facts.** "To review" looked up the meters of each of the 10,000 things (66 ms with 53
  meters); it is now one hashed set (93 → 10 ms).
- **The agenda's first page** read the view twice (counts, then the page); it is one read now.
